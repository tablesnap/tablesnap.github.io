The problem with copy-paste

Pasting a web table into Excel usually goes wrong: merged cells, footnote marks like [12] glued to the numbers, “1,234” stored as text, and long tables that you have to scroll and select by hand.

TableSnap reads the table directly from the page and builds a proper spreadsheet. It also works on things that look like tables but aren't – product grids, search results, rankings and lists of cards.

Step by step

  1. Open the page with the tableAny website works: Wikipedia, sports or financial statistics, government data, price lists, your own dashboards.
  2. Open TableSnapClick the TableSnap icon in Chrome's toolbar or press Alt+Shift+T. The side panel opens on the right.
  3. Click the tablePress “Pick table or list on page”, point at the table until it is outlined in green, and click. If the outline is too small or too big, press ↑ or ↓ to adjust it.
  4. Check the columnsThe preview shows the first rows. Click a column title to rename it, or hide columns you don't need under “Columns”.
  5. Download ExcelPress “Download Excel” for a .xlsx file with a bold, frozen header row – or copy it straight into Google Sheets or Excel.

Tables split across pages? If the site has a “Next” or “Load more” button, press “Auto-paginate” and TableSnap collects every page into one file, skipping duplicate rows.

Tips

  • Several tables on one page? Run TableSnap once per table – each capture becomes its own file.
  • European number formats (1.234,56) are recognised, so figures stay numbers in your Excel.
  • Need the data in ChatGPT or Claude? Use “Copy for AI” to paste it as a clean Markdown table.
  • Your last capture is kept if you close the panel, so you can come back and download it later.

Questions

Is there a row limit?

No. The free plan exports any number of rows to Excel, CSV and Google Sheets.

Why are my numbers text when I paste a table into Excel?

Copy-paste keeps the formatting of the website, including thousand separators and footnote marks. TableSnap cleans each cell and writes numbers as numbers in the .xlsx file.

Does it work on pages that need a login?

Yes. TableSnap reads the page you are looking at in your own browser, so it works on dashboards and intranets you are signed in to. Nothing is sent to us.

Can I export to CSV instead of Excel?

Yes. You can download CSV (it opens correctly in Excel, with accents), copy for Google Sheets, or copy as Markdown or JSON.

What if the table isn't an HTML table?

Most “tables” on modern websites are built from cards or lists. TableSnap detects repeating patterns and turns each item into a row anyway.
